About

# about.py

I'm a Machine Learning Engineer at the Cyprus Marine and Maritime Institute, where I build the AI behind live maritime systems: detection, segmentation and tracking across cameras, LiDAR and weather sensors, deployed on GPUs, Jetson devices and Tenstorrent accelerators.

My work covers the full ML lifecycle. I engineer the data, train the models, design and profile the inference pipeline, and integrate it into operational platforms that people rely on in a control room.

On the data side I work with very large spatiotemporal datasets: satellite imagery, high-resolution biodiversity images, and around 15 TB of AIS vessel signals, using Polars, DuckDB and partitioned Parquet.

chord_colours/

Seeing Sound: guitar chords as colour

A tool that helps deaf and hard-of-hearing people play the guitar. A classifier listens to the guitar in real time and recognises which notes are sounding.

Each note has its own colour. When you play a chord, the colours of its notes blend into one, so every chord gets a distinct colour you can see and learn without hearing it.

PythonAudio classificationReal-timeAccessibility
my_coding_assistant/

Function Guardians

An experimental coding assistant for Python repositories. It indexes every function and gives it a persistent semantic card covering its purpose, inputs and outputs, invariants, dependencies, tests and known failure modes.

When code changes, it reviews the git diff against those cards and the call graph, flags broken contracts, suggests card updates and proposes tests. It runs from the CLI, in CI with stable JSON output, or inside VS Code through an MCP server, with local models via Ollama.

plate_sr/

Low-res plate recognition (ICPR 2026)

My entry to the ICPR 2026 Competition on Low-Resolution License Plate Recognition. Each vehicle comes as five consecutive low-resolution frames, so pixelated that the characters can't be read, and entries are scored on exact-match recognition rate.

I trained a super-resolution model that reconstructs the plate at high resolution so a recogniser can read it. In most examples the characters come back close to the real plate, with the remaining errors on look-alike pairs such as 8 and 9, or 0 and 8.
